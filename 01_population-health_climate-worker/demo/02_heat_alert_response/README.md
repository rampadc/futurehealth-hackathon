# 02. Stop exposed work when the local heat limit is reached

The civil crew is doing heavy outdoor work when Sandline's civil-area monitor reports a valid reading of 145 W/m² at 8:00 a.m. That reaches the site's heavy-work hold band. The existing beacon alerts the area, but it cannot establish whether Huda Al Nadir, the civil foreman, received the instruction, whether work actually stopped, or where the workers went. Dune loses output while the task is held, and the crew needs to know how required recovery will be treated. The response must be traceable across those questions.

## Follow the response

The [interactive presenter guide](scenario-2-new-ui.html) covers the current UI's core response and full journey, including persona tabs, live planning and employer review. Download and open the standalone HTML in a browser. It is excluded from the application image and is not a route in the deployed app; private rehearsal evidence is retained separately.

1. Show the reading's civil-area source, measurement time, quality and units, then show the applicable hold rule. Maya Al Qamar, the safety officer, records the hold at 8:01 a.m. under Rashid Al Wadi's site safety authority. The assistant can connect evidence to the rule and show the required next actions. A recorded hold is still an instruction, not proof that the crew has acted on it.
2. Huda acknowledges the alert at 8:02 a.m. This shows she has taken ownership of the message. At 8:03 a.m. she separately confirms that heavy civil work stopped. Keeping those entries apart means a later reviewer can see the difference between receiving an instruction and carrying it out.
3. At 8:04 a.m. Huda records eleven civil workers in the recovery shelter. The site can account for them and provide water, rest and cooling. A shelter headcount is evidence of recovery, not permission to return to the exposed task.
4. Huda can explain Dune's approved Heat Rest Schedule: required recovery in an ordinary rostered shift is protected paid rest for covered civil workers. The stop and shelter entries support a time record, but they do not prove a payroll payment. Individual pay questions go to Dune's employer channel.

Rashid's safety instruction and Huda's protective action do not wait for a cost agreement or for a worker to refuse unsafe work. Workers still have their own route to report symptoms or unsafe conditions. Amal Al Nadir, the project manager, takes the separate Dune–Sandline cost question for later review. The Heat Rest Schedule does not determine subcontract reimbursement or a client allowance.

## Where the case stands

The alert is acknowledged, cessation is confirmed and recovery is recorded. Heavy civil work remains held. Current local conditions, people, welfare, permits and a human restart decision must be reviewed before the instruction changes.
