# 01. Plan the shift before the heat arrives

Sandline has exposed civil work planned at its Mussafah utility corridor and a truck booked to deliver aggregate: sand, gravel or crushed stone used in construction. The civil crew needs a workable morning plan, while the yard needs to receive the load. At 8:00 a.m., a forecast points to a hot period from 11:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. in the civil and yard areas. That warning creates time to coordinate, but it does not measure the temperature or heat stress at either work front.

## Follow the planning discussion

1. Open the work plan and delivery book together. Heavy civil work and material receipt share a schedule dependency, so a change to one can affect the other. The forecast is background for this discussion; it does not by itself clear the crew to work or put them on hold.
2. At 8:01 a.m., Maya Al Qamar, Sandline's safety officer, records a recommendation to review the timing of exposed heavy work. The proposed 8:00–11:00 a.m. window would avoid the forecast peak and the 12:30–3:00 p.m. outdoor work restriction. The assistant can show the proposal, source and assumptions. It has not changed the roster or booked a new delivery time.
3. Rashid Al Wadi, the superintendent, owns the site decision. He needs to review the plan with Huda Al Nadir, the civil foreman, and Noura Al Nadir, the logistics coordinator. Huda can check crew readiness, acclimatisation, water and cooling; Noura can check the yard's receiving capacity and the supplier booking. Those conversations may produce a revised sequence, but no revised schedule or supplier agreement appears in this record.
4. Before exposed work proceeds, the team still needs a fresh, valid local civil-area reading and the normal task, people and permit checks. A later local hold would override a morning planning suggestion. A regional forecast cannot stand in for current work-front evidence.

The early recommendation helps the team avoid being surprised by the forecast peak, while leaving authority where it belongs. If changing the work or delivery creates costs, Amal Al Nadir, the project manager, owns that later discussion. Neither output pressure nor a possible supplier charge makes an uncertain local condition safe.

## Where the case stands

The record contains a forecast and Maya's recommendation. It contains no approved rescheduling, supplier acceptance, local clearance or stop instruction. The next decision depends on the site team's review and current conditions.
