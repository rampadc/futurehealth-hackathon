# 09. Review the evidence before restarting

Heavy civil work begins in a held state. A new local heat reading at 8:00 a.m. shows 175 W/m², an improvement from the hold band. That may make a restart review possible, but it is not a new instruction to the crew. The site still needs to know whether the instrument is current and healthy, where the people are, whether water and rest arrangements are ready, whether the permit is in order, and whether the midday outdoor restriction applies. A past decision cannot be reused if those conditions change.

## Follow the restart decision

1. At 8:01 a.m., Huda Al Nadir, the civil foreman, tries to restart the heavy task. The action is rejected because a current human review has not been recorded. The assistant can surface the better reading and missing gate; it cannot treat the improved number as automatic permission.
2. At 8:02 a.m., Rashid Al Wadi, the superintendent, reviews the local reading, person accountability, water and rest, permit and midday restriction. Tariq Al Safa, the permit coordinator, supports the permit check through the site process. Rashid's review is an inspectable human decision, with the conditions tied to the time of action.
3. At 8:03 a.m., Rashid records an authorised restart while those checks are satisfied. The crew can now see who changed the instruction and on what evidence. Amal Al Nadir's later review of costs caused by schedule changes remains separate from this safety authority.
4. Six minutes then pass. At 8:10 a.m., another restart attempt is rejected because the civil reading is stale under Sandline's five-minute limit. The earlier review and earlier permission do not make the old measurement current. Failed instruments and the 12:30–3:00 p.m. outdoor restriction are also gates that must be checked at the time of any future action, even though this event sequence does not show those separate branches occurring.

The two attempts illustrate different reasons for saying no: first the necessary human review is missing, then the environmental evidence is too old. Neither rejection should be hidden by the successful reviewed restart between them. The assistant's useful role is to show the changing evidence and the decision owner, while the superintendent and permit process retain authority.

## Where the case stands

A reviewed restart was recorded at 8:03 a.m. under then-current conditions. The later stale-reading attempt was blocked. Any further work decision needs a fresh local measurement and current review of the relevant gates; a previous approval is not a standing all-clear.
