# 08. Respond to a worker's symptoms

Bilal Al Safa reports dizziness during a separate civil-area incident. The local monitor has a current valid reading of 175 W/m², outside Sandline's automatic heavy-work hold band. That reading describes an area condition; it cannot establish whether Bilal is well enough to continue. The worker's report triggers the site's first-aid route in its own right. Treating the environmental band as reassurance would miss the person who needs attention.

## Follow the person

1. At 8:00 a.m., the civil-area reading is recorded with its time, quality and source. It is useful context for the incident, but there is no automatic environmental hold from this value. The assistant can show that distinction without telling the crew that the worker is safe.
2. At 8:01 a.m., Huda Al Nadir, the civil foreman, records that Bilal reports dizziness. The shared entry uses only that minimal observation. It is not a diagnosis, and it should not become a performance label or a speculative account of why he feels unwell.
3. Bilal is removed from the active task and the trained first aider, Salim Al Wadi, is contacted. This protective response follows the symptom report rather than waiting for a lower heat reading or a general crew hold. Huda remains responsible for crew accountability while the first-aid route takes over the individual response.
4. At 8:02 a.m., Salim records a trained assessment and referral decision. The event establishes that a responder acted; it does not specify a diagnosis, treatment outcome, medical clearance or completed follow-up. More sensitive details belong in the first-aid record, not the shared crew log.

The assistant can link the report, removal and responder action so that the handoff is visible. It must not infer a medical conclusion from the symptom or the monitor. Bilal can seek help without a general alarm having sounded and without a commercial discussion first. If absence or wages later become an issue, Dune's employer process handles them separately from the site incident record.

## Where the case stands

The symptom incident and first-aid response are recorded, and Bilal is no longer treated as available for the active task. Follow-up remains open where the available record does not show completion. There is no medical clearance or invented diagnosis. This branch is independent of the connected civil hold, workshop and material delivery case.
