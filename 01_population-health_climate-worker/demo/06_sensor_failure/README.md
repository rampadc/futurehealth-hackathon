# 06. Say when local conditions are unknown

Sandline's civil-area heat monitor fails while the team still needs to know whether exposed civil work can proceed. The electrical-area instrument continues to report a current reading, and the failed civil instrument retains an older value. Both are tempting shortcuts under programme pressure. Neither is a current, reliable measurement of the civil work front. The useful answer for Huda Al Nadir's crew is that local conditions are unknown until Sandline verifies them.

## Follow the failed reading

1. At 8:00 a.m., Maya Al Qamar, the safety officer, records the civil monitor failure. Keep the affected instrument and area visible, along with the last civil reading as history. A historical number can explain what was known earlier; its presence in the register must not make the current status look clear.
2. At 8:01 a.m., the electrical-area monitor reports a valid reading of 195 W/m². Maya tries at 8:02 a.m. to use that reading to clear the civil area. The check rejects it because the instrument serves another zone. The assistant can identify the mismatch; it cannot transfer a favourable band across work fronts.
3. Replay time advances by six minutes. At 8:09 a.m., Maya tries the old civil instrument's value. The check rejects this too: the instrument has failed and the reading is beyond Sandline's five-minute freshness limit. The two failures have different reasons, and both should be shown rather than collapsed into a vague warning.
4. Maya and Huda use the manual verification and protective route while Rashid Al Wadi, the superintendent, arranges a local assessment. Any affected work remains subject to that process. The record does not show a replacement reading or an authorised clearance.

The assistant's job here is to make the missing evidence apparent, including the zone, time and instrument status. It cannot manufacture a measurement from the forecast or infer the civil condition from a neighbouring crew. Amal Al Nadir may later review actual delay and cost, but pressure to keep the schedule moving does not make an invalid input reliable. A new verified local measurement and the required human review are the next steps.

## Where the case stands

Both clearance attempts are rejected. The civil work-front condition is unknown; the old value remains historical, and the electrical value remains specific to its own area. There is no all-clear or restart decision in this record.
