Reviewed 13 November 2026. I clicked through all 16 stages of Josh's interactive tutorial, including the worker check-in/offline switch, missing acknowledgement, timeline scrubber, both red-flag answers, both Leila replan branches, all three Planning Agent questions, and the restart evidence checks. I compared that observed journey with the current desktop demo source and its supplied layout screenshot. This is a UX comparison, not a requirement to reproduce the tutorial's visual design or to turn a local demo into a live safety system.

**Verdict:** The current app captures the central human handoffs and the held-work/crew-plan/restart separation. It is a credible role-based walkthrough, but it does not yet tell the same *causal* story as clearly. The largest issue is that its handover waits for restart review, reversing the tutorial's open-decision handover. The next most valuable changes are to expose the evidence and eligibility behind the proposals and to make the assistant's limited, local role legible.

## Highest-value fixes for this demo

1. **Correct handover order.** Let Rashid hand over the stopped trench, current crew/recovery plan, delivery status and unresolved restart prerequisites before the separate restart review. Show the incoming supervisor the named open decisions. Keep the site restart decision outside the app.
2. **Put decision evidence next to conclusions.** On Maya's and Rashid's screens, show the reading source/zone/time, legal band versus fictional stricter site limit, task scope, freshness, policy source and complete restart checklist. Use the captured fields already validated by `replay.ts`; avoid suggesting that recording a button generated new sensor or solver evidence.
3. **Explain proposal eligibility and blocked states.** On Huda's screen, show the qualification/readiness/capacity funnel, why Leila is excluded or included, the owner of each missing confirmation, and the resulting 90/120-minute replan difference. Preserve the distinction between a proposed assignment and Huda's recorded confirmation.
4. **Make the assistant's scope explicit.** Rename or describe it as a local case guide in this demo, attach source links to the relevant case view, and give a direct purpose-limitation refusal for worker ranking/contracts. Do not claim agent grounding/citations/telemetry that the browser-only implementation does not have.
5. **Add a short orientation and closeout.** A case overview and final “system proposed / person recorded / still open” summary would connect the nine account switches into one morning. Keep worker voice, offline notices, actual supplier messaging and live authentication as clearly planned or out of scope.

## Boundaries and evidence

- The tutorial itself labels components as built, partly built or planned, and states that all solver outputs are proposals (`applied: false`). Its mock screens are explanatory, not a production design specification.
- The current `README.md` states that this is a local, offline, desktop-first story layer over nine independent prerecorded captures. Account selection is simulated; sign-out/sign-in changes persona within the tab; refresh resets the case. There are no live notifications, sensor feed, ADK/Gemini call, supplier message, real approval or persistent audit trail. The review does not treat these deliberate boundaries as defects.
- Specific implementation evidence: `src/story.ts` (actors, prerequisites, handoff order), `src/App.tsx` (role workspaces, proposal/restart panels and scenario selection), `src/Timeline.tsx` (minute inspection), `src/AssistantChat.tsx` (local replies), and `src/replay.ts` (captured-case loading and validation).

## Whole-app critic and changes after review

A separate whole-app critic found that the awkward appearance mostly came from decision hierarchy and repetition, rather than inconsistent MUI styling: generic greetings outranked tasks, large future-step cards competed with current work, and the green environmental band lacked an adjacent explanation of the stricter task rule. The fixed right rail also consumed space for little information.

The implementation now uses task headings and a bounded content width; presents the heat reading beside the returned heavy-work restriction; collapses unavailable restart/handover steps; avoids repeating the current actor’s handoff; and uses a contextual assistant side sheet with a compact composer and links to supporting case views. Delivery sending is explicitly a recorded milestone. Handover no longer requires restart review or supplier acceptance and shows the decisions carried forward. Focused tests cover this open-decision handover.

The original comparison table describes the reviewed baseline. Those improvements do **not** close every gap. Eligibility/exclusion explanations, the complete restart checklist, timed evidence in authored activity, and orientation/closeout still need richer treatment. The assistant remains local deterministic logic. Login/logout is intentionally retained at the user’s request; role switching is not changed into a dropdown or presented as production authentication in developer documentation. No worker-phone, live messaging, cloud, sensor or real approval functionality was added.
