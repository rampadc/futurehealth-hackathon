01 · Core metaphor

The issued sheet.

Heat-Safe is read in a site cabin and in 45 °C glare by people who already trust two things: engineering drawings and safety signs. Every screen is a sheet on the site permit board. Drawing conventions decide structure; safety-sign conventions decide colour. Nothing floats, glows or casts a shadow, because a sheet doesn’t.

  1. 1 · Line weight is hierarchy.Hair 1px separates rows. Structure 2px frames a section. Signal 8px frames, on all four sides, the one cell that needs action.
  2. 2 · Line style is certainty.Solid = issued fact. Dashed = draft, proposal or AI, not yet issued. Hatched = no work happens here.
  3. 3 · Ink fill is a stamp.A solid ink block means a human recorded it, or an emergency. Stamps are the heaviest mass on a sheet, so they are rare.
  4. 4 · Colour is a safety signal, never decoration.ISO 3864 meanings only: red prohibits, yellow cautions, green confirms a safe condition, blue instructs. Components are identified by number, not hue.
  5. 5 · A condition is not a permission.The environmental band (a swatch) and a task hold (a stamp) are drawn differently, so “145 W/m² · Low band · heavy work stopped” can never be misread.
  6. 6 · One focal point per sheet.The focal readout runs at 36–56px; everything else stays at 24px or below. That gap in scale is how the eye finds what to do next.

02 · Colour

Ink, paper and four signals

The palette is taken from safety signage (RAL signal colours, tuned for WCAG), not a UI kit. Ratios below are computed live for the current light setting.

Base

Don’tUse a signal colour for a component, a chart series, a hover, a brand moment or a “nice” accent. Yellow is never text.
Don’tUse Tailwind-default hues, zinc or slate greys, or shadows. Greys here are warm graphite, matching drafting paper.

05 · Motion

Mechanical, never floaty

Colour and state change instantly. Things that are recorded stamp (140ms, stepped scale). Things that arrive wipe in from the reading edge (180ms, four steps, like a plotter pass). Loading is a hatch that travels. Nothing bounces, fades or eases out slowly. With reduced motion everything is instant.

06 · Icons

One drawing rule

24×24 grid, 2px stroke, square caps, miter joins, no fill. Directional icons mirror in right-to-left; play, clock and safety glyphs never do. The AI diamond is the only dashed icon.

11 · Action rows

The workhorse of every list

One row is one touch target. The lead is the crew photo whenever the row is about a person. The trailing stamp carries status; the inline-start edge carries selection (ink); urgency closes the whole row in the 8px signal frame. On the worker app descriptions never truncate.

13 · Feedback states

Errors, offline, empty, loading

No happy-path-only screens. Errors never clear a form; cached data stays visible with its age. Empty states say why the sheet is blank and offer one way forward.

Couldn’t reach Heat-Safe in 20 seconds. Showing the last update from 08:04. Your entries are saved on this device.

14 · Confirmations

Friction that matches the stakes

Supervisors at a desk type the action word (CONFIRM, RESTART). Anyone on a phone in the field, and every worker, uses hold-to-confirm: no keyboard, no Latin script, and no reading needed under stress. Both use the native <dialog>, so focus is trapped and Escape closes it.

18 · Glare and gloves

Built for 45 °C sun and work gloves

Glare is the default for the site and worker apps outdoors. It is pure black on white, with every text pair at 11:1 or better (WCAG AAA asks for 7:1). Tinted surfaces turn white, rules thicken to 2/3/6px, body text goes up to 18px at weight 500, and drafts rely on their dashed edge alone. Switch the Light control to Glare to see the whole guide in it. Night shift keeps the warm palette; nobody needs glare protection at 04:00.

19 · Offline and sync

Saved first, sent when possible

Every record is written to the phone first; the network only decides when it leaves. The sync indicator sits in every title block (try the compositions above), and the queue shows each unsent record, its state, and what the person can do about it. Safety alerts never wait for sync: they come from the rules on the phone.

    20 · Agent task state

      22 · Guardrails and rollback

      Friction by stakes; rollback by withdrawal, not erasure

      Every record keeps a visible way back. Safety records are never silently undone: they are withdrawn, which keeps them in the audit log and re-raises whatever they had cleared. A stop-work notice that has already reached phones can’t be unsent; withdrawing it sends a follow-up.

      23 · Field capture

      Photo, voice, yes/no before any keyboard

      Evidence in the field is captured, not typed. Photos are for equipment and conditions (meters, coolers, shade), never people. Voice is transcribed and the audio discarded. Binary questions are two big buttons. Everything lands in the sync queue first.

      24 · Zone plan

      Anchor actions to places, not people

      A 2D site plan anchors bands, holds, sensors and actions to zones, so a supervisor sees where the problem is. It deliberately shows counts, never a dot for each worker: Heat-Safe is not a location tracker. Zones are keyboard buttons; the detail follows selection.

      25 · Company branding

      The company signs the sheet; it doesn’t colour the signals

      Each company can bring its palette to two surfaces, set separately: the platform (supervisor web) and mobile web (site app, worker app and shelter kiosk). Brand colour goes in identity slots only: the brand bar, the logo mark, the browser theme colour and an optional faint tint on the rail. brand.js checks every colour against the four signals and the contrast floor of each light setting. When it has to adjust a colour, it changes lightness and keeps the hue.

      1. 1 · Identity slots only.Brand bar, logo mark, browser theme colour and rail tint. Nothing in the content column: stamps, bands, statements, buttons, links, focus and charts never take a brand colour.
      2. 2 · Signal-like colours stay in the mark.Red, orange, yellow and green brand colours, and anything almost identical to a signal, never fill a bar. They stay in the small logo mark beside the company name. “Auto” falls back to the secondary colour, then to paper.
      3. 3 · Contrast is fitted per light setting.Bar text reaches 4.5:1 in day and night shift and 7:1 in glare. Night shift dims bright bars to 40% lightness so nothing glows in a dark cabin.
      4. 4 · Safety screens drop the brand.A stop-work or emergency statement removes the strip and turns the browser colour to ink. Not configurable.
      5. 5 · Heat-Safe stays named.The bar always carries the product line under the company name, so people know which system issued the sheet.
      6. 6 · Platform and mobile web are set separately.The desk can carry a full bar and a rail tint. The phone gets a 32px strip, or the browser colour only, because screen height is scarce and glare is the default outdoors.

        26 · Crew photos
        A face beside every name
        
      
        Wherever a row, readout, stamp, message or task belongs to a named person, a small round crew photo sits in front of the name. On a busy site people find a face faster than they read a name, so the eye links content to a person before reading. The circle is the system’s one round shape. Nothing else is round, so a photo can never be mistaken for a control, stamp or signal.
