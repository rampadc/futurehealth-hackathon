# Physical Health Companion — business and hackathon plan

**Decision memo · 3 November 2026 · concept, not a validated product or purchase commitment**

**Execution in one minute.** Build one working three-button pet and clinic-reader demonstration by **20 November**; register for the Future Health challenge by **10 November**. Show the distinctive return-after-lapse flow with a smaller pre-approved action, explicit “pain → pause,” and honest event provenance. Seek **one paid provider feasibility pilot** after separate usability and safety work: 100 optional companion kits and 100 usual-care comparator participants, subject to approvals. The clinic is the lead channel; consumer retail is a separate later offer. At an assumed AED 300 kit price, AED 185 delivered/support cost and explicit comparison, tooling and approval allowances, the pilot-only year-one case has a **minimum modelled AED 263,500 funding gap** before payment timing or quote overruns. No purchase, hardware build, efficacy result or partner is secured.

## Executive opportunity

Abu Dhabi has a real programme in which sustained activity matters: the Department of Health (DoH) and Abu Dhabi Public Health Centre (ADPHC) launched the Personalised Weight Management Programme in June 2025 for eligible Thiqa adults, with a 16-week initial plan, coaching, nutrition and activity guidance, clinically indicated treatment, follow-up, and the Sahatna app. A 2 November 2026 programme update says more than 6,500 people benefited in its first year across more than 54 facilities and reports 14% average weight loss among participants, rising above 22% among those committed for a full year. These are programme-reported results, with no published comparator, completion denominator, week-four attrition, or causal estimate for its digital tools. [Launch](https://www.mediaoffice.abudhabi/en/health/department-of-health-abu-dhabi-and-abu-dhabi-public-health-centre-launch-personalised-weight-management-programme-first-of-its-kind-in-middle-east/) · [First-year update](https://www.mediaoffice.abudhabi/en/health/launched-by-department-of-health-abu-dhabi-and-abu-dhabi-public-health-centre-abu-dhabi-personalised-weight-management-programme-benefits-6500-individuals/)

The opportunity is a *hypothesis*: some participants may respond better to a tangible, friendly object that welcomes them back after missed activity than to another phone notification. The proposed Physical Health Companion is a three-button, offline-first virtual pet that turns clinician-approved movement into small adventures, keeps ordinary play available, and never punishes a lapse. A clinic reader provisions the plan and retrieves consented, source-labelled activity summaries. This is a distinct product bet, not a feature already shown to work. The team’s [concept specification](../ideation/Physical-Health-Companion-Concept-and-System-Design.pdf) sets a 30-day battery target and five evidence types, but neither the device nor its effects have been validated.

**Recommendation:** pursue an adult provider-led discovery and pilot in the Weight Management ecosystem, while preserving the physical companion as the core experimental treatment. Sell no reimbursement or weight-loss promise. Start with a research or service-improvement agreement at one willing clinical provider, using a working physical prototype only after usability and technical gates. Treat a software simulation as a way to test rules and language, not proof that an object changes behaviour. DoH/ADPHC may be a later public buyer; procurement authority and budget ownership must be discovered. Schools are an attractive second segment because the pet may fit children better, but their consent, safeguarding and incumbent wearable requirements make them a separate product and evidence programme.

## Buyer, user and payer: do not collapse them

The *user* is an eligible adult participant who elects to receive the companion. The prescribing or coaching team is the daily workflow owner: it defines safe activity ranges, answers exceptions, and decides whether a report helps treatment. A clinic or provider network could sponsor a limited pilot; DoH/ADPHC could authorize scale or shape standards; Thiqa’s third-party administrator is involved in the medication coverage pathway. These roles are not evidence that any one entity will procure kits. The June 2025 DoH reimbursement policy applies specifically to obesity medication coverage for eligible adults and to licensed providers and Thiqa’s administrator. It requires sustained activity, documented compliance, and a synchronized report in Malaffi; it recommends a wearable measuring steps and heart rate connected to Sahatna, with documented exceptions for some patients. A clip-on motion-only pet cannot be offered as the required monitoring device without approval, validated measurement and an accepted integration route. [DoH policy, §§3.2–3.6](https://www.doh.gov.ae/-/media/9F8611EF0B7841F28E2AAE4614327F59.ashx)

That policy gives the buyer a concrete reason to care about sustained, documented activity, but it also makes this a difficult sale. The companion's first job should be motivational support and a separate, clearly labelled observation stream. Its report must never turn self-report or game currency into policy compliance. If a provider wants reimbursement use, specify the exact data fields, accuracy standard, Sahatna/Malaffi integration and approval path before development. Sahatna already handles health records, insurance, dependents, notifications and optional linked wearables. Its role as system of record is an advantage to the programme, not a gap that the companion should claim to replace. [Sahatna FAQ](https://sahatna-app.doh.gov.ae/en/faqs/) · [Sahatna privacy notice](https://sahatna-app.doh.gov.ae/en/privacy-policy/)

For the participant, the task is to restart an approved routine after disruption without a guilt cue. A clinician may value earlier discussion of barriers and fewer unplanned silent drop-offs, but neither outcome has been measured locally. Some adults may reject pet aesthetics entirely; the pilot must offer a discreet visual theme and measure voluntary continued use rather than assume acceptance. The person can pause, decline sharing beyond care, and keep ordinary play. The delivery organisation bears provisioning, training, lost-unit, charging, accessibility and support costs; a licence price that ignores those costs is not a buyer case.

A provider reviewing its 2027 service design has a reason to ask whether a low-friction adjunct improves participation, but this is an inference, not an announced purchasing process. ## Offer and competitive reality

The offer is a programme-bounded adjunct: physical pet device, staff provisioning reader, approved activity templates, Arabic/English and accessible interaction design, local event ledger, source-labelled summary, and support/replacement service. Its strongest potential differentiation is a tangible, immediate, non-punitive interaction that remains usable day to day without opening a phone, combined with clinician-defined limits and honest event provenance. Walking estimated by motion, a completed timer, a participant confirmation and a clinical measurement must remain separate. The device should not prescribe, infer calories, assess exercise form, certify weight loss, or automatically grant medical coverage. Its simple hardware is meaningful only if the emotional and practical benefit survives comparison with products already available.

The scientific prior is encouraging but small. A 2024 meta-analysis of 36 adult trials found gamified health apps added about 489 daily steps versus non-gamified versions and modest weight/BMI effects; it did **not** test this offline pet or prove long-term weight-management savings. A 2025 meta-analysis of 16 child/adolescent trials found a small increase in moderate-to-vigorous physical activity, with no significant daily-step effect. A 2025 obesity trial of a gamified app with telecoaching found larger step gains than usual care, but the combined intervention cannot isolate a pet or a physical device. The correct claim is that behavioural game elements merit testing, not that this design has established efficacy. ## Price and economic test

Price the **full service**, not a USD 69 retail analogy. For a paid feasibility pilot, propose a fixed research/service fee that covers study setup, Arabic/English content, staff training, device support, analysis and secure data handling; show separately a capped per-kit transfer price after obtaining manufacturer quotes. A scale contract might have per-issued-kit hardware, one-time site onboarding and a modest reporting/service fee, but no recurring charge should be assumed until the buyer accepts its value. The earlier USD 24 landed hardware, USD 69 retail and USD 30–50 licence figures are unsupported placeholders. Get quotes for enclosure, board, display, battery, assembly, certification, shipping, spares and warranty; test battery life under Abu Dhabi heat and real button use before any margin claim. The [concept PDF](../ideation/Physical-Health-Companion-Concept-and-System-Design.pdf) contains only an arithmetic power budget, not measured battery performance.

For buyer value, predefine an incremental 16-week completion or objectively measured activity effect against usual care plus a cheap tracker/reminder. Suppose, **only to illustrate a threshold**, 325 people receive AED 300 kits: kit spend is AED 97,500 before training and service. A 10 percentage-point absolute increase in completion produces an expected 32.5 extra completers, or AED 3,000 kit cost per extra completer (AED 97,500 ÷ 32.5). At five points the figure doubles; at no lift it is unbounded. The buyer must state the monetary or clinical value of one additional sustained participant, and the trial must estimate the effect; the 10-point threshold is an aspiration, not evidence. Medication reimbursement should **not** be counted as vendor ROI unless the approved data route and actual payer economics are verified. A cost-effective alternative may simply be extra coaching hours or a better reminder. Compare total cost per additional completer, not just device unit cost.

## Distribution: how a kit reaches a person

**Lead channel — clinic-issued adjunct.** Start with one weight-management service, not a government-wide contract. Sheikh Khalifa Medical City (SKMC) is a plausible *discovery target* because its published Diabetes and Endocrinology Center includes medical obesity management, lifestyle programmes and an Endocrine Bariatric Clinic. Cleveland Clinic Abu Dhabi's Obesity Medicine Clinic is a second plausible target with obesity/lifestyle medicine, specialist nurses and patient education. SEHA CLINICS publishes endocrinology and obesity-management services that could be a later network route. Their websites establish relevant care capabilities; they do **not** establish participation in this specific DoH programme, buying authority, interest in this product, or a relationship with the team. [SKMC](https://skmc.seha.ae/diabetes/) · [Cleveland Clinic Abu Dhabi](https://www.clevelandclinicabudhabi.ae/en/specialties/comprehensive-obesity-metabolic-center) · [SEHA CLINICS](https://clinics.seha.ae/specialty/endocrinology/)

At enrolment, a trained coach offers the optional device only to an adult whose clinician has approved a simple activity plan. The clinic issues a charged, uniquely numbered kit and a one-page Arabic/English explanation. The staff reader loads signed allowed actions and limits; the person chooses theme, quiet hours and whether to share a summary. At review, staff see a brief *source-labelled* trend and barriers the person chose to disclose, with the usual record and wearable still authoritative. The clinic's incentive is a testable chance of better sustained participation and more useful coaching conversations; the clinician's incentive is a report that takes under two minutes to interpret. The participant gets an enjoyable object without an extra phone task. Contracting must compensate the clinic's onboarding, review and support time rather than treating it as free distribution.

**Second channel — consumer retail after clinical and product fit.** A separate, entertainment-first SKU could be sold through a direct storefront and, if a buyer agrees, a retailer such as Virgin Megastore UAE, which already lists fitness trackers. Its role would be discovery, merchandising and returns; it would not confer clinical endorsement or access to patient data. A retail SKU needs ordinary wellness claims, self-selected goals, retail margin, consumer warranty and support, and a clear boundary from a clinician-issued plan. Do not count retail demand in the clinic forecast. [Virgin Megastore UAE tracker category](https://www.virginmegastore.ae/en/electronics-accessories/smart-watches-health-fitness/activity-trackers/c/n010602)

**Supply and fulfilment options, not commitments.** Request low-volume board and enclosure quotes from a manufacturer such as Seeed Studio Fusion, which lists PCB assembly, component sourcing and 3D printing; compare a second quote and test assembled samples. For larger UAE distribution, Aramex advertises warehousing, last-mile delivery and returns, but a clinic may prefer its own controlled issue/return process. Neither firm has quoted this design or agreed to work with the team. Manufacturing choice follows BOM, battery, reliability, data and import reviews; a maker-service lead time is not a promise that a finished health product can ship by the hackathon. [Seeed Fusion services](https://support.seeedstudio.com/knowledgebase/articles/947004-what-is-fusion) · [Aramex UAE](https://www.aramex.com/ae/en/home)

**First customer → first 100 kit users.** The first sale sought is one *paid* feasibility agreement with a provider service owner and a clinical sponsor, after approvals. Before that study, test 20–30 voluntary adults on physical usability and reliability; they are **outside** the randomised comparison, and any prototype changes must be frozen before the comparison begins. The proposed clinic study then invites eligible adults and randomises 200 consented participants 1:1: **100 receive the companion plus usual care; 100 receive the same usual care without the companion**. Both arms receive the same approved tracker/reminder and independent measurement. Clinic staff use a standard invitation and identical clinical follow-up scripts; allocation is concealed until enrolment, crossovers and shared-device use are logged, and analysis keeps everyone in their assigned arm. If workflow contamination is substantial, redesign the comparison with the provider and statistician before launch, potentially assigning by clinic or intake period. This is a first distribution unit and a feasibility estimate, not a powered efficacy claim. Record the offer/decline denominator, reasons for refusing a pet, activation, staff minutes, support tickets, replacements, missing data and follow-up in **both** arms. Seek a written decision on whether a larger powered trial is worth funding, not an implied rollout.

**Sales and procurement path.** (1) A 20-minute demo with the obesity service lead and patient educator establishes the exact failed routine and current alternative. (2) A discovery session with operations, information security, clinical governance and procurement maps data flow, intended use, device ownership and budget. (3) A 2–4 week protocol/design phase defines endpoints, independent measurement, ethical approval route, kit and staff cost **for both study arms**, and a signed pilot statement of work. (4) Deliver a paid **100-kit/200-participant feasibility pilot** with milestone billing and service-level terms. (5) Review uptake, directional outcome estimate, safety and total cost against pre-agreed feasibility thresholds; separately size and fund the powered efficacy study needed for an outcome claim. (6) Only then discuss a second-site evaluation or conditional 500-kit commercial order through the provider's normal purchasing process, making the remaining efficacy uncertainty explicit. DoH/ADPHC endorsement or Malaffi integration is a separate decision. Budget owner, tender route and cycle time remain discovery items. Supplier registration alone does not ensure a government award. [Abu Dhabi Government Procurement Gate supplier guidance](https://adgpg.gov.ae/Trading-With-Government/Becoming-a-Registered-Supplier)

**Delivery promise.** Price includes staff training, a ten-minute initial issue workflow target, an accessible Arabic/English guide, replacement stock, help contact, defect triage and an agreed lost-device policy. A return or replacement must revoke the old reader pairing and keep the person's clinical plan under provider control. Collect only minimum activity events; keep patient identity at the provider where feasible; obtain explicit consent for any transfer; document retention, deletion and incident handling before clinical use. Abu Dhabi's ADHICS guidance covers health-information security for entities handling such data, and the DoH HIE standards govern any proposed clinical exchange. A wireless production model also needs TDRA type-approval assessment; medical-device classification depends on final intended use and claims. These are work packages and costs, not a claim of certification. [ADHICS implementation guidance](https://www.doh.gov.ae/-/media/Feature/Resources/Guidelines/ADHICS-v2-implemetation-guideline.ashx) · [DoH HIE standards](https://www.doh.gov.ae/en/resources/HIE) · [TDRA equipment approval](https://tdra.gov.ae/en/services/equipment-registration)

## Year-one commercial arithmetic (illustrative, AED)

This is a cash planning model for the **first 12 months after 3 November 2026**, not a quote, order, or forecast. It assumes one paid **100-kit/200-participant feasibility study** at AED 35,000 service fee and a *conditional* second-site 500-kit order at AED 45,000 onboarding/reporting fee, both with AED 300 per issued kit. The second order is a stretch commercial decision under explicitly unresolved efficacy, **not** an inference that the feasibility study proved a clinical effect. It must not be sold as a powered efficacy study; such a study needs its own sample-size calculation, comparator budget and contract. No recurring subscription is assumed. Per-kit cost is an unverified **AED 125** board/display/battery/enclosure/assembly and QA, **AED 25** freight/handling, **AED 15** spares/warranty provision and **AED 20** support: **AED 185** total. Get a detailed BOM and two landed quotations before pricing.

The base **AED 190,000** operating allowance comprises **AED 70,000** engineering/firmware, **AED 35,000** protocol and evaluation-analysis labour, **AED 45,000** founder or sales labour/travel, and **AED 40,000** privacy, regulatory *assessment*, insurance and operations. Add **AED 15,000** reader fixtures and tooling, **AED 30,000** preproduction testing and approval-process allowance (separate from assessment, with no approval assumed), and **AED 10,000** for the separate 20–30-person usability/reliability phase. The 200-participant comparison adds **AED 50,000** for recruitment, staff time, follow-up and independent outcome collection in *both* arms (200 × AED 250) and **AED 15,000** for common tracker/reminder or reference-measurement provision in *both* arms (200 × AED 75), even if a provider later supplies some of these in kind. Normal clinical care is the existing baseline, but its **incremental study workload is budgeted**. These are unquoted minimum planning allowances, **AED 310,000 fixed for the pilot year**, and may miss site-specific research fees, import duties, full production certification or a larger sample. A conditional second-site order adds **AED 20,000** setup/training labour, making its case **AED 330,000 fixed**. Obtain manufacturer, reader, testing, certification and research-delivery quotes before committing to prices or a production order.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Only paid 100-kit/200-participant feasibility pilot | 100 × 300 + 35,000 = **65,000** | 100 × 185 = **18,500** | **310,000** | **–263,500** |

Contribution is **AED 115 per kit** before the one-off study and fixed costs. With only the pilot fee, arithmetic operating break-even at this fixed cost would need `(310,000 – 35,000) ÷ 115 = 2,392` issued kits (rounding up). With both service fees and the extra site cost it would need `(330,000 – 80,000) ÷ 115 = 2,174` kits. Neither is a validated scale forecast: more sites, comparative research, staffing, readers and certification would increase cost. The pilot-only case needs **at least the modelled AED 263,500 funding gap** if this full scope is committed and all assumed invoices are collected; add a cash buffer because components, approvals and study work may be paid before clinic receipts, and actual quotes may be higher. Negotiate a pilot deposit and milestone billing and order in small batches. The AED 35,000 service fee is plainly below the assumed cost of running and preparing this comparison; seek a provider/research co-funding commitment, a higher fee, a narrower approved scope or defer the study. A clinic's value case must separately cover its own staff time and show an incremental benefit over usual care. This is a costed *test of a sale*, not a claim of year-one profitability.

## Hackathon path: make the physical thesis demonstrable by 20 November

The selected statement is **Sustaining Healthy Behaviors, Challenge 09**, verified through the interactive [challenge page](https://hack2skill.com/event/futurehealthchallenge/). See the [shared competition strategy](../../00_cross-cutting/FHDIC_Ideation_Assessment_Business_Plans.md) for dates, eligibility, assessment dimensions and submission terms. The team's differentiator is the **physical moment of return**: a companion at hand welcomes the person back and offers a smaller clinician-approved action without a streak reset. The demo must show that bounded adaptation, not merely a cute step counter.

**Minimum credible build.** Build one safe handheld bench prototype with three real buttons, a small display (or visibly attached development display), a motion input and local storage. A laptop reader provisions a signed *demo* plan with two allowed activities, quiet hours and an upper bound, then retrieves an event ledger that distinguishes motion estimate, elapsed timer and self-report. An event-state test should reject an out-of-bounds change and a forged/duplicate sync. Use synthetic adult records only. A battery-life target, custom PCB, certified enclosure, clinical integration, 16-week impact and AI model are **not** submission dependencies; represent each as a later gate. The simulator is valuable for deterministic state tests and screen capture, but by itself cannot demonstrate tactile use, actual sensing, carry comfort or battery behaviour. If a physical board is unavailable, submit the honest simulator plus an enclosure/interaction mock-up and a candid hardware gap; do not imply the mock-up has validated the physical thesis.

**Three-minute live demo storyboard (a rehearsal cut, not an official time limit).** 0:00–0:30: show an adult's clinician-approved, hypothetical walk plan and existing Sahatna/tracker context; state the unmet question: getting back after a lapse. 0:30–1:00: issue the actual device through the reader, choose Arabic or English and quiet hours, and show the signed bounds. 1:00–1:30: press the device's buttons to complete ordinary pet care, then trigger a short movement event; the pet explores while the ledger labels the input as an estimate. 1:30–2:10: advance a **clearly marked simulated clock** past missed days, choose “too tired” or “bad weather,” and show a shorter, already-approved action offered with no lost progress. Choose “pain” and show pause/clinician review rather than automatic exercise advice. 2:10–2:40: attempt an unsafe goal increase and rejected reader sync; show participant opt-out and a source-labelled summary. 2:40–3:00: show the proposed clinic pilot offer, cost per kit and the planned 16-week comparator, with all unmeasured claims labelled. Record a backup video of the same actual build and a one-page architecture/data-flow diagram. Expand or shorten this cut to the organiser's assigned presentation slot.

## Pilot, gates and next decisions

**0. Buyer and participant discovery (next 4–6 weeks).** Interview a small mix of weight-management clinicians, administrators and eligible adults across Arabic/English use, existing tracker ownership and differing comfort with pets. Seek baseline retention and activity data by enrolment cohort, workflow map, data-sharing constraints, budget owner and a clinic willing to evaluate. Obtain written permission before accessing patient records. Confirm whether the provider can run a service-improvement evaluation or needs research ethics approval. Ask at least one school programme owner about the official RFQ and safeguard requirements, but keep that track separate.

**1. Rule and usability prototype.** Demonstrate the three-button journey, signed plan bounds, pause/return behaviour and labelled evidence in a simulator. Then build a small physical batch for 20–30 voluntary adults **outside the later randomised study**. Predefine success as comprehension without staff rescue, acceptability across both languages, a short setup time acceptable to staff, no harmful lapse response and no serious data/provisioning defect. Collect dissenting views, especially from adults who decline the device. Freeze the tested physical and software version before the comparative cohort starts; a simulator pass is a design gate, not an efficacy claim.

**2. Technical gate.** Measure battery life in realistic heat, active use and ageing scenarios; validate walking estimates and wear detection against a reference tracker; test lost-device, reader authentication, offline sync, deletion and tamper cases. The original 30-day target remains valuable only if the measured device meets it without excessive size or price. Pre-specify the acceptable sensor error and support rate with the provider. If the physical prototype cannot meet safety, durability, privacy and cost requirements, compare a purchased tracker plus companion app or retire the hardware concept.

**3. Controlled 16-week feasibility study.** With consent and required ethics clearance, randomise 200 eligible adults 1:1 after the separate usability phase: 100 usual-care-plus-companion kit users and 100 usual-care-only participants. Both arms retain identical approved tracker/reminder provision, clinical contact and independent outcome measurement; the pet is the only intended treatment difference. Record allocation, crossovers and contamination, do not withdraw standard treatment, and analyse by assigned arm with missingness reported. Pre-register a sustained-behaviour measure (for example, weekly independently measured activity through week 16) and separately measure programme completion, time from lapse to return, device wear, staff time, adverse experience, lost units and provider-collected clinical measures. Pet opens and self-reports are secondary process data. The 200-person study tests recruitment, acceptability, measurement, safety and the direction/uncertainty of effect; it is **not powered or sold as definitive efficacy**. A later powered trial requires a statistician's sample-size plan and separate financing before any effectiveness or rollout claim.

**Go/no-go.** Advance from feasibility to a **funded powered comparison**, rather than a clinical effectiveness claim, only if uptake, directional incremental signal, staff burden and privacy/safety are acceptable and a buyer or research sponsor commits to the study cost. A second-site commercial order before that evidence is an explicitly uncertain, buyer-approved evaluation, not a validated rollout. If outcomes improve but hardware fails, test the behaviour rules on an existing device. If neither incremental behaviour nor buyer willingness emerges, stop the institutional hardware thesis. No clinical outcome, procurement award, integration or market size should be presented as secured before these gates.

## Evidence ledger and corrections to earlier briefs

The existing [business brief](BUSINESS_OPPORTUNITY.md) and [pitch](../pitches/PITCH_BRIEF_Physical-Health-Companion.md) are useful concept history, but this memo supersedes their commercial assertions. **Verified:** official programme launch and first-year results; DoH medication policy requirements; Sahatna’s existing features; Healthy Horizons/Pura; ADEK school policy framework; official Ultrahuman research MoU; published research on related gamification. **Secondary-only:** ADEK and ADPHC wearable tender listings and their dates. **Unknown:** local week-four drop-off, any actual kit budget, buyer authority, total eligible subgroup, unit cost, companion efficacy, device classification, integration permission, and willingness to pay. The old briefs’ “week-four leak,” 20–80k seats, reimbursement payoff, first-to-market gap, and retail/licence economics are hypotheses or unsupported figures. Their claim that phone users, children and seniors are simply missed by current platforms is too broad; Sahatna manages dependents, Pura assists seniors, and Garmin already offers child activity adventures. The Ultrahuman relationship is a validation-stage MoU, not proof of live Sahatna functionality. These corrections preserve the original insight: a tangible, non-punitive pet may have real value, but only a fair comparison can show where and for whom.
