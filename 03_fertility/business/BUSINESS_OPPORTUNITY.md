# Sitr (ستر) — Business brief

**Idea:** A private front door that gets men into Abu Dhabi reproductive care early — screening and referral, not a home lab.  
**Narrative:** `../pitches/PITCH_BRIEF_Sitr-Male-Reproductive-Front-Door.md` · **Research:** `../research/`  
**Status:** Concept. No contract. Sell partnerships, not a tender.

---

## The line

Abu Dhabi built the reproductive system (screening, genetics, IVF, genomics). Men still arrive late, and ashamed. Global products sell a semen test. The open budget is the step **before** the test: a private conversation that ends in a licensed appointment.

**Payback rule:** Price a **completed, appropriate consult**, not app downloads. One consult a clinic would not have got from a billboard is worth more than a free habit app. The clinic sets the margin; we take a navigation fee.

---

## Who pays

**Start here:** one DoH-aligned programme **or** one licensed IVF / andrology group. There is no open Abu Dhabi “male fertility app” tender.

| Who | Why they act now | What they buy |
|---|---|---|
| **DoH reproductive programme** | Strategy says men should engage earlier. The front door is still thin. | Programme licence + governed reporting |
| **IVF / andrology clinic** | Late male-factor cases and no-shows. Ads feel like a fertility waiting room. | Fee per completed booked consult |
| **Primary care / premarital clinics** | Genetics is structured. Everyday male reproductive intake is not. | Workflow licence |
| **The man (later)** | Wants privacy | Free check-in; paid teleconsult with a licensed doctor |
| **Employers and insurers** | — | **Do not sell.** A punitive data product kills trust. |

**Why now:** Premarital genetic screening is already mandatory for the Emirati pathway (570 genes / 840+ conditions). Clinics (Fakih, Cleveland Clinic Abu Dhabi, others) are busy at the back end. The Future Health challenge is a public invitation for this front door.

---

## Market

**Sell into:** patient entry for a system that already treats people. Not mail-order diagnostics.

| | Sketch |
|---|---|
| What exists | Large private IVF market; male factor is a large share of infertility worldwide |
| What we can reach first | Thousands of men a year on premarital, preconception, and early-fertility paths in Abu Dhabi — only with a partner |
| Year 1 money | A programme licence (mid five figures, placeholder) + a per-consult fee. Small until trust exists. |

Do not pitch a venture-scale consumer app. Pitch a design partner and a micro-pilot.

## Competitors

| They sell | Players | Why we still win |
|---|---|---|
| A home sperm test | YO (~USD 100), ExSeed, SpermCheck | Starts at the sample. Feels like a diagnosis. Weak path into Abu Dhabi clinics. |
| Mail-in lab and freezing | Legacy, UK postal kits | High commitment. Wrong country. Not “check in years early.” |
| A daily habit score | Motily, Juno, Finn | Easy to build. Weak clinical safety. Easy for judges to dismiss. |
| The clinic itself | Fakih, Cleveland Clinic Abu Dhabi | Excellent once you walk in. The walk-in is the problem. |
| Premarital genetics | ADPHC | Keep it. We do not replace it. |

**Positioning:** Kits answer “what is in this sample?” We answer “should I talk to someone, privately, and who?”

## Battlecards

### YO / ExSeed

- **They:** Private phone-camera sperm screen. Cleared in major markets. Repeat kits.
- **Gap:** The man must already be ready to produce a sample. “Normal / abnormal” can feel like a diagnosis. Not an Abu Dhabi care route.
- **Say:** “A kit can be a later step. We own the conversation before the sample.”
- **Don’t say:** “Home tests are bad.”

### Legacy (mail-in + freeze)

- **They:** Real lab numbers and long-term storage.
- **Gap:** Cross-border, expensive, high commitment.
- **Say:** “Freezing is downstream. We are how someone gets there locally, if they should.”

### Habit apps (Motily and similar)

- **They:** Two-minute daily check-ins tied to sperm health.
- **Gap:** Wellness content. Little red-flag handling. No licensed Abu Dhabi referral.
- **Say:** “Habits can be a module. Triage and a human appointment are the product.”

### Local IVF clinics

- **They:** Diagnosis and treatment. Some already use lab AI.
- **Gap:** Stigma before the first visit. Female partner often investigated first.
- **Say:** “We send a consented, structured referral. We do not replace the lab.”
- **Don’t say:** “Clinics are failing men.”

### Premarital genetic screening

- **They:** The state’s process. High trust. High stakes.
- **Gap:** Not a general male health check-in.
- **Say:** “We prepare the man for that conversation. We never override the result.”

## Offer and plan

1. Cultural and clinical review of every sentence (pass/fail).  
2. One partner: programme owner or clinic.  
3. Prototype judged on: catches red flags, never says “you are infertile.”  
4. Small pilot: did he finish, did he attend, did he trust it.  
5. Genomics and Malaffi only after that. Not in the first product.

**12 months:** signed design partner → safety review passed → ethics-ready pilot → a fee per completed consult agreed in principle.

## Objections

**“YO already sells to men.”**  
They sell a sample. We sell an early, local, governed appointment. Different budget (clinic acquisition vs a box).

**“No tender means no buyer.”**  
This is a partnership sale. The strategy and the clinics are the buyer. The challenge is the door.

**“A questionnaire isn’t a business.”**  
The business is completed consults, not quiz completions. If referrals don’t happen, there is no fee.

**“You’ll be sued.”**  
No diagnosis in the product. Red flags go to a person. If the copy overclaims, the build fails review.

**“Just add genomics. That’s the Abu Dhabi story.”**  
Later. A genomic consumer app before trust is how this brand dies.

**“Clinics don’t need leads.”**  
They need leads men will accept. Billboard IVF does not do that.

**“Revenue is tiny.”**  
At pilot scale, yes. Raise against the partner and the pilot, not against a GCC TAM slide.
