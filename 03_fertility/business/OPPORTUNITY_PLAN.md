# Sitr — business and hackathon plan

**Opportunity plan — 3 November 2026.** Concept and discovery proposal, not a clinical service or a forecast. The first commercial test is with one licensed provider; a public programme is a possible later buyer. All monetary figures and funnel rates below are explicit hypotheses, not observed Abu Dhabi demand.

**Execution summary.** Build an inspectable, bilingual, privacy-preserving *referral preparation* prototype for the male reproductive health track by 20 November. Show how user-entered signals lead to understandable next-step options, a consented history packet and human review, with synthetic cases and measurable safeguards. The hackathon build is separate from a paid live product: after the challenge, seek one licensed provider with a demonstrable booking gap, then budget AED 75,000 for engineering/QA hardening before an approved AED 10,000/12-week pilot. Its first 100 invitations test feasibility, not annual return; an AED 24,000/site/year licence requires a larger prospective comparison and a buyer-specific economic case. Even the upside year-one scenario has a modelled AED 69,000 cash shortfall, so funding and staged spend are part of the go/no-go decision. No partnership, clinical efficacy, revenue or challenge success is claimed.

## Executive opportunity story

Abu Dhabi launched its Programme for the Future of Reproductive Health in September 2026. Its published scope includes prevention and preconception care, genetic screening, fertility diagnosis and treatment, ART, and preservation; it explicitly describes shared responsibility for men and women. This creates a credible setting in which to ask whether men have an easy, trusted way to raise reproductive concerns before or during clinical care. It does **not** establish that a digital intake is missing, funded, or wanted by providers. [Abu Dhabi Media Office, 17 September 2026](https://www.mediaoffice.abudhabi/en/health/abu-dhabi-programme-for-the-future-of-reproductive-health-an-integrated-ecosystem-shaping-standards-research-and-innovation/)

The clinical need is plausible and substantial. WHO estimates that 17.5% of adults worldwide experience infertility during their lifetime, while cautioning that country and cause-specific data have gaps. This global lifetime measure cannot be multiplied by Abu Dhabi's population to calculate an addressable market. In an infertility evaluation involving a male partner, the 2026 ASRM opinion says the partners should be assessed concurrently, with a male reproductive history and at least one semen analysis at the outset where applicable. AUA/ASRM guidance likewise calls for concurrent assessment and expert evaluation when findings suggest male infertility. These sources support timely access to qualified care, not a questionnaire that diagnoses fertility or a universal preconception testing campaign. [WHO, 2023](https://www.who.int/news/item/04-04-2023-1-in-6-people-globally-affected-by-infertility); [ASRM, 2026](https://www.asrm.org/practice-guidance/practice-committee-documents/fertility-evaluation-of-infertile-women-a-committee-opinion-2026); [AUA/ASRM guideline](https://prod.asrm.org/practice-guidance/practice-committee-documents/diagnosis-and-treatment-of-infertility-in-men-auaasrm-guideline-part-i-2020)

Sitr's proposed wedge is a discreet Arabic/English **navigation service**, offered through a licensed clinic or primary-care partner: a man can privately describe a concern, see carefully reviewed educational information, choose whether to share a concise history, and reach the right human appointment. The initial promise is narrower than “early detection”: fewer abandoned or misdirected first steps among people already seeking help. The product should never label someone fertile or infertile, infer an inherited risk from a questionnaire, or substitute for semen analysis, examination, or genetic counselling. Clinical review must decide whether any symptom-based urgency routing is safe and lawful before it appears to users.

The business case is **unproven**. A provider might value appropriately attended new consultations, a clearer referral history, lower staff handling, or a better patient experience. A patient may value privacy and clarity. Neither group has yet shown willingness to pay for Sitr. A small, governed pilot can test behaviour, safe workflow and cost inputs; a larger comparison is needed before a credible buyer-ROI or procurement story.

## Hackathon build and demo plan

Enter **Male Reproductive Health, Challenge 01** on the [official challenge page](https://hack2skill.com/event/futurehealthchallenge/). The implementation below is our proposed response, not an official scorecard or a prediction of selection. See the [shared competition strategy](../../00_cross-cutting/FHDIC_Ideation_Assessment_Business_Plans.md) for dates, eligibility, assessment dimensions and submission terms.

**Build the smallest defensible product.** A mobile web flow presents an Arabic/English language choice, a neutral title and a private-use warning before collecting anything. It offers an education-only path with no account. An opted-in path asks for the concern, relevant history and any user-supplied test *description*; it must distinguish a reported result from an uploaded, verified clinical result. A deterministic, versioned rules layer maps individual answers to **explainable topics for discussion** and a next-step *option*, never a fertility score or diagnosis. Each explanation has (a) the specific user answer that activated it, (b) a clinician-reviewed source/copy version, (c) what remains unknown, and (d) a clear statement that a clinician determines tests and treatment. The user can correct answers, see the complete share packet, omit optional fields, choose contact preferences and explicitly send it to a simulated clinic inbox. A staff view shows what was actually shared, a provenance trace and a human status of accept, redirect or request clarification. Every demo record is synthetic; a clear delete action and retention timer are visible.

**Technical differentiation from a questionnaire.** The prototype is an auditable *handoff engine*: a separation between input, educational signal, next-step rule, consented payload and human disposition. Use a small rule manifest with IDs, rationale, language copy versions and review status; a replayable trace makes the same synthetic inputs produce the same output. The staff can override a suggestion with a reason; the audit compares suggested versus accepted route. Maintain an allowlist of approved sentences rather than free-form generation of clinical advice. If an LLM is used at all, limit it to nonclinical copy drafting outside the user runtime; do not let it invent routes or citations. This creates a concrete technical story around inspectability, reversibility and workflow measurement without claiming novel medical prediction.

**Safety boundary.** A screen can explain that sudden/severe symptoms need prompt direct medical help, but the prototype must not classify urgent cases from unreviewed questions. Any symptom-specific red-flag rule stays disabled until a qualified clinical reviewer approves wording, escalation owner, availability and fallback; the demo may show the *governance switch* and a generic immediate-care escape path. No real identifiers, genetics, semen results, clinic API or live appointment slots are needed. If the demo runs locally or in a private sandbox, mark it “synthetic demonstration” and verify that its endpoints and assets are not publicly retrievable. A clinical reviewer may veto content and the team should show the changed rule version, not silently patch it.

**Exact three-case demo storyboard, about seven minutes if the organiser permits.**

1. **Private entry (60 seconds):** A synthetic Arabic-speaking man with a new concern opens from a neutral provider link, views the education-only option and the plain-language consent screen. Show that no sensitive analytics identifier appears and that ordinary booking stays available.
2. **Explainable signal (90 seconds):** He supplies a synthetic history of a prior undescended testis and uncertainty about future fertility. [AUA/ASRM guidance](https://www.auanet.org/documents/Guidelines/PDF/Male-Infertility-Guideline.pdf) treats such reproductive history as relevant to evaluation; the demo only displays a topic for clinician discussion, the exact answer that activated it, source/copy version, uncertainty, and a choice to request an andrology or primary-care conversation. It never labels him infertile or recommends a test.
3. **Consent and human handoff (90 seconds):** He removes one optional answer, chooses a safe contact method, reviews the packet, and sends to a simulated inbox. The staff view shows that excluded content is absent, plus a suggested route and human override control. Reopen his view to show consent withdrawal/deletion.
4. **Two countercases (90 seconds):** Replay a synthetic person trying to conceive as a couple; show simultaneous partner evaluation information and no male-only blame. Replay a person describing sudden severe symptoms; show the general immediate-care escape and the disabled, unreviewed red-flag rule rather than a false automated triage result.
5. **Proof and business (90 seconds):** Display the synthetic test dashboard, one reviewed rule trace, privacy checks, and a one-page provider pilot protocol with baseline comparison, buyer, fee and stop criteria. End on the specific decision requested from a provider: access baseline and a protocol sponsor.

**Pre-submission evaluation is prototype performance, not clinical efficacy.** Create 24 synthetic cases before testing, with common, ambiguous, missing-data, Arabic/English and adversarial wording variants. Freeze expected *workflow* properties before running them: all cases produce a trace; no case emits “fertile/infertile” or treatment advice; user-omitted fields never appear in a share packet; repeat runs produce identical next-step outputs; and each urgent-symptom countercase exposes a direct-care exit without an automated clinical classification. Target **24/24 trace and repeatability checks, 0/24 forbidden claims, and 0/24 withheld-field leaks**; report actual failures, not only the targets. In a small moderated session of six to eight *non-patient volunteers*, counterbalance a generic direct-booking mock-up and Sitr on equivalent fictional tasks. Measure time to find a human next step, task completion, what each participant believes will be shared, and perceived privacy in each language; report numerator/denominator, task order and limitations, not a population statistic or a claim about any named provider's live form. If a qualified clinician reviews the cases, report agreement on routing and any disagreements as design feedback; without one, explicitly label routing **unvalidated**. No live patient outcomes, sensitivity/specificity, diagnostic accuracy or reduced time-to-care claim is possible by 20 November.

One person can cover multiple roles; a clinical reviewer can be consulted only under appropriate terms. **Fallback:** if none is available, remove symptom-specific routing and any “clinician approved” label, show the review queue and unresolved content, and submit the technically complete synthetic workflow as unvalidated. If no provider agrees to talk before submission, use public examples only to explain a *proposed* pilot and do not imply a letter of intent. If engineering time slips, preserve consent, trace, share preview and human override; cut account creation, integrations, at-home testing, AI chat and custom analytics first.

**Pitch deck, recommended six slides:** (1) the real access decision and current booking comparator; (2) private Arabic/English user journey and clinical boundary; (3) live three-case handoff demonstration; (4) architecture, provenance, consent and human override; (5) synthetic test results with failures and remaining clinical validation; (6) first buyer, first 100-invitation feasibility cohort, AED 10,000 pilot, live-product hardening budget and conditional AED 24,000 licence. Structure the spoken pitch around a short demo with time left for questions; check final organiser instructions rather than treating timing as fixed.

**Likely judge questions and evidence-led answers.** “Why is this more than a form?” → show the versioned rule trace, consent-controlled packet and staff override, then the evaluation matrix. “Where is AI?” → no AI is required for technical depth; deterministic rules are deliberate where a clinical claim would be unsafe, and future AI would need its own validation. “Will this detect infertility?” → no; it prepares an appropriate human discussion and the prototype does not validate diagnosis. “Why would a clinic buy?” → only if an expanded prospective comparison later establishes enough incremental appropriate attendance or verified staff savings to cover fees, setup and live-product costs; the first 100 invitations only test feasibility. “How will you reach men?” → provider-owned, optional link at an existing inquiry; no partner is claimed. “Can you deploy?” → only after funded engineering/QA hardening plus clinical, privacy, security, procurement and local regulatory review; synthetic demo is not a live service. “Does it work in Arabic?” → demonstrate the flow and report language-specific comprehension results, with local review still required.

## Whose problem, who decides, who pays

The **user** is an adult man in Abu Dhabi who chooses to ask about future fertility, a current difficulty conceiving, sexual or reproductive symptoms, a treatment that may affect fertility, or a clinician's recommendation. A partner may be involved only at the user's choice. A separate route for an infertile couple must preserve parallel assessment of both partners; Sitr should not quietly make the man the sole cause or the woman the default patient. The clinical guidelines describe the couple-based pathway, but they do not measure local stigma or digital uptake. [ASRM, 2026](https://www.asrm.org/practice-guidance/practice-committee-documents/fertility-evaluation-of-infertile-women-a-committee-opinion-2026)

The **first buyer candidate** is the manager of a licensed fertility/andrology provider who controls patient access and service operations, with an andrologist or reproductive urologist as clinical sponsor. Procurement, privacy and information-security teams would also need to approve. This buyer has a plausible operational lever: an existing appointment channel can invite a private, optional intake and compare its results with normal booking. Cleveland Clinic Abu Dhabi already offers a men's health programme, male infertility services, appointment requests, a portal and WhatsApp contact; Fakih IVF advertises fertility urology and andrology services. Sitr would have to improve an existing route, not create the first route. [CCAD andrology](https://www.clevelandclinicabudhabi.ae/en/specialties/andrology-clinic-men-health); [CCAD appointments](https://www.clevelandclinicabudhabi.ae/en/appointments/request-an-appointment); [Fakih IVF andrology](https://fakihivf.com/treatment/fertility-urology-andrology/)

The **second buyer candidate** is a DoH-aligned reproductive-health or primary-care programme that values equitable access and measured navigation quality. That is a longer, more governed sale with a different outcome framework and no verified budget line for Sitr. Abu Dhabi Public Health Centre already runs premarital screening and says genetic testing became essential for Emiratis intending to marry in the emirate from 1 October 2024. Its page names 22 centres and a defined counselling process. Sitr must not imply that premarital genetics is a fertility test, insert itself into that mandatory process without permission, or count its participants as Sitr leads. [ADPHC premarital screening](https://www.adphc.gov.ae/en/Public-Health-Programs/Premarital-Screening)

The **payer** in the first pilot should be the provider or a research/innovation sponsor, with no charge for the digital intake. User-paid consultations, insurance reimbursement, and public commissioning are separate hypotheses requiring coverage, licensing and procurement checks. Employers should not receive individual reproductive information; an employer-funded route would introduce a serious trust and consent problem without evidence of demand. No identifiable data sale or advertising is part of the offer.

## The offer and the boundary of clinical responsibility

Version one is a partner-branded or neutrally branded mobile web flow reached from a provider's current site, appointment desk or a clinician-given link. It explains what the service can and cannot do, offers an anonymous educational path, asks for the minimum history needed to route an opted-in appointment, and lets the user confirm exactly what is sent to the provider. A human partner owns clinical advice, tests, urgency decisions, records and follow-up. The referral packet contains user-entered concerns and contact preferences, not a machine-generated diagnosis or probability of infertility. The service should support Arabic and English copy that has been reviewed by local clinicians and people from the intended user groups.

If the clinical sponsor wants urgent-symptom prompts, they must approve the content, escalation hours, handoff owner, failure mode and audit process. Until that review, the safe prototype uses only general instructions to seek appropriate medical care and avoids claims of triage accuracy. For couples already experiencing infertility, the flow should make it easy to book a concurrent evaluation rather than present lifestyle advice as a substitute for clinical testing. The AUA/ASRM guideline warns that semen parameters alone do not reliably establish fertility or infertility; a questionnaire is an even weaker basis for that claim. [AUA/ASRM guideline](https://prod.asrm.org/practice-guidance/practice-committee-documents/diagnosis-and-treatment-of-infertility-in-men-auaasrm-guideline-part-i-2020)

The proposed intended use is a service workflow for education, history collection and appointment navigation, without a diagnostic claim or laboratory function. That design choice does **not** establish an exemption from medical-device or other health-service rules: classification and permitted claims remain for the partner and relevant regulator to determine before a live pilot. No at-home sperm analysis, gene interpretation, ranking of clinics by success rate, or direct Malaffi integration is in the first test. Technical controls should include least-privilege access, defined retention, consent records, and a clear deletion and incident process. DoH's 2024 healthcare information and cyber-security guidance says its scope includes technology and service providers that handle Abu Dhabi health information; the exact obligations and hosting design need local counsel and partner security review before identifiable data is collected. [DoH ADHICS implementation guidelines, 2024](https://www.doh.gov.ae/-/media/AD67C27DDEDB466A80444A24E3106E76.ashx)

**Differentiation to prove:** a lower-friction, culturally acceptable path from private concern to an appropriate *attended* appointment, with explicit consent and a usable history for staff. It is a hypothesis about workflow and trust, not proprietary clinical insight. A provider could copy the forms. The defensible asset, if any, would be measured conversion, clinical governance, local language design and integration into a real pathway. If the partner's existing booking process performs as well, Sitr should not be bought.

## The market we can actually reach

There is no defensible local TAM in the present archive. WHO's global infertility prevalence is a measure of need, not annual purchase intent. The 22 premarital centres are infrastructure, not a channel agreement or a count of interested men. Nor is the programme's stated network of more than 250 facilities a list of prospective Sitr customers; it spans many types of care. The first market boundary is therefore **one to three consenting provider sites**, adults already arriving through those sites' own channels, and a limited number of months. [WHO, 2023](https://www.who.int/news/item/04-04-2023-1-in-6-people-globally-affected-by-infertility); [ADPHC premarital screening](https://www.adphc.gov.ae/en/Public-Health-Programs/Premarital-Screening); [Abu Dhabi Media Office, 2026](https://www.mediaoffice.abudhabi/en/health/abu-dhabi-programme-for-the-future-of-reproductive-health-an-integrated-ecosystem-shaping-standards-research-and-innovation/)

The following is a **capacity sensitivity**, not a forecast. “Eligible visitors” means adult people at a participating access point with a self-declared reproductive concern, after duplicates and people requiring immediate conventional care are removed. Site counts, monthly volumes, opt-in, appropriate referral and attendance rates are invented *model inputs* to be replaced by partner data. The result counts appointments, not infertility diagnoses, ART cycles or births.

| Illustrative case | Sites × eligible visitors/site/month × 12 | Opt in | Appropriate appointment among opt-ins | Attend | Annual attended appointments |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Cautious | 1 × 60 × 12 = 720 | 20% | 40% | 65% | ~37 |
| Working | 2 × 80 × 12 = 1,920 | 30% | 50% | 70% | ~202 |
| Expanded | 3 × 120 × 12 = 4,320 | 40% | 50% | 75% | 648 |

The table tests *capacity*, not revenue. The earlier AED 10,000/year plus AED 50 per incremental appointment formulation has been superseded: AED 10,000 is the **12-week pilot** price anchor, followed by a proposed **AED 24,000/site/year fixed licence** only if the pilot meets the buyer's thresholds. No referral or treatment-linked fee is in the initial offer. At the working-case ~202 attended appointments across two sites, AED 48,000 in annual licence fees would equal ~AED 238 per attended appointment, before any adjustment for incrementality; this is an illustrative unit-cost check, not a quoted cost of acquisition. A clinic must compare the licence with *incremental* appropriate visits and staff time saved, not all visits.

## Distribution: one provider, then the first 100 users

**Start with a clinic-embedded route.** The first target type is a provider with both male reproductive expertise and an appointment operation that can measure drop-off. Publicly visible *candidate conversations*, not partnerships or ranked leads, include [Fakih IVF Abu Dhabi's fertility urology/andrology service](https://fakihivf.com/treatment/fertility-urology-andrology/), [Cleveland Clinic Abu Dhabi's andrology clinic](https://www.clevelandclinicabudhabi.ae/en/specialties/andrology-clinic-men-health), and [Burjeel Hospital Abu Dhabi's men's health clinic](https://burjeel.com/abu-dhabi/promotions/mens-health-clinic-at-burjeel-hospital-abu-dhabi/). Each already offers care, so the specific sales question is whether optional private preparation improves its own access funnel. Choose **one** only after an access manager can provide a baseline, a clinician can sponsor content, and a privacy owner can specify a permitted path; name recognition alone is not a reason to select a site.

| Route | Entry and incentive | Control point and conflict | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clinic embedded | Optional link on the provider's men's health/booking page, in its appointment confirmation, or offered by a call-centre agent. Provider may gain appropriate attendance and less repeated history-taking. | Provider owns appointment capacity and records; users may distrust a tool that seems to steer them toward profitable treatment. Show the ordinary booking link beside Sitr; report referral appropriateness and complaints, not IVF conversion. | **First 12-week test**, contingent on site consent and baseline. |
| Primary care/referral | A family physician can offer an optional preparation link after a reproductive concern is raised. [SEHA Clinics publicly lists family medicine, urology and male infertility services](https://clinics.seha.ae/). | Primary care has a broader reach but more institutional approvals; no automatic access to public screening participants, records, or referrals. Maintain physician choice and neutral specialty criteria. | Second channel after a clinic pilot shows safe handoff; buyer and procurement route must be identified separately. |
| Direct to consumer | Private search/educational page or a trusted community referral could reach men before a clinical inquiry. | High acquisition cost, weak clinical handoff, and strong privacy concerns. No sensitive ad audiences, retargeting pixels, lookalikes, fertility-keyword profile sharing, or sale of reproductive data. | Discovery only. Do not budget paid acquisition or claim a consumer funnel in year one. |

**First provider sequence.** Build a short prospect map from public service pages; request a 30-minute discovery with the access owner and andrology sponsor only when outreach is authorised. Ask for four weeks of de-identified inquiry→booking→attendance counts by existing channel, staff minutes per inquiry, misroutes, appointment capacity and a named contracting authority. Run a 15-minute workflow demonstration using only synthetic data. If the provider finds a material, addressable gap, propose the protocol and AED 10,000 fixed pilot; if the issue is unavailable appointments, solve capacity rather than sell an intake. This is a proposed sales process; no contact or agreement is implied.

**First 100 users, conditional on an approved live pilot.** Invite 100 consecutive eligible adults from the provider's existing men's health/urology inquiry or booking stream, with ordinary booking available at the same point. A provider staff member gives a neutral optional link; Sitr does not import patient lists or contact people itself. Use a daily denominator log: eligible, invited, opened, consented, completed, shared, booked, attended, and opted out. At the working-case assumptions, **100 invitations → 30 opt-ins → 15 appropriate appointment suggestions → about 10–11 attended appointments** (100 × 30% × 50% × 70% = 10.5), with *incremental* attendance lower still. At 30% opt-in, obtaining 100 *opt-ins* would require roughly 333 eligible invitations before completion loss; the immediate pilot can instead cap at 100 **invited** and learn the actual rates. Arabic and English scripts, phone type, referral source and declined reasons should be reviewed without exposing individual histories. A 12-week period with 60 eligible inquiries/month yields ~180 invitations if all are suitable; the site must validate that capacity before promising a 100-invitation sample. This small cohort tests feasibility, comprehension, privacy and workflow; it cannot establish the **75 incremental appropriate attended visits per site per year** needed for clinic break-even under the model below. No community outreach, employer channel or premarital programme access is presumed.

**From demo to contract.** Discovery produces a baseline and buyer-owned problem statement. A synthetic demo earns approval to write a protocol, not access to patient data. The paid 12-week pilot requires a signed scope, clinical content owner, privacy/security review, allowed hosting, incident and deletion plans, explicit consent, measurement design, and a patient-facing alternative booking route. A week-six check examines safety, complaints, and funnel integrity; a week-12 report estimates appropriate attended first visits per eligible invite versus the existing route, with uncertainty stated. Procurement then chooses stop, revise, or an **expanded, prospectively designed comparison** whose duration and sample size follow the observed baseline and minimum useful effect. The first 100 invitations cannot justify an annual ROI or conversion claim. An annual licence is an upside scenario after stronger evidence on incrementality and site economics; rollout adds a site only after localisation, staff training, capacity and audit ownership are confirmed. A public programme would require its own procurement route and outcomes, not a clinic contract transferred by assumption.

## Alternatives and the reason to act now

The most important competitor is **the current route**: direct appointment booking, call centres, clinician referral, primary care, and existing men's health campaigns. Cleveland Clinic Abu Dhabi's MENtion It campaign explicitly encourages men to raise concerns, so a generic “men won't talk” pitch is too broad. A pilot must establish where this route loses users and whether a private intake improves it. [CCAD MENtion It](https://www.clevelandclinicabudhabi.ae/en/campaigns/mention-it); [CCAD appointments](https://www.clevelandclinicabudhabi.ae/en/appointments/request-an-appointment)

**YO Home Sperm Test** sells a self-test of semen parameters; its current product listing offers three tests for US$99.95, and the US FDA lists a 2024 clearance. **ExSeed** offers a home sperm test and app; its current shipping policy lists Europe, the United Kingdom and Canada. **Legacy** provides mail-in semen testing and storage and states that it is available in all 50 US states. Neither cited page establishes UAE availability. These are real alternatives for motivated consumers or models a provider could emulate, but none proves a local clinic will pay for Sitr. Home testing may also be a useful downstream option if a licensed clinician recommends it; the pitch should not denigrate it. [YO product](https://yospermtest.com/products/yo-box); [US FDA YO record](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfPMN/pmn.cfm?ID=K241628); [ExSeed shipping policy](https://shop.exseedhealth.com/policies/shipping-policy); [Legacy service availability](https://www.givelegacy.com/about)

**ADPHC premarital screening** addresses infectious and inherited-disease risks and supplies formal counselling. It is an adjacent public service, not a Sitr competitor to displace. **Fakih IVF and Cleveland Clinic Abu Dhabi** already diagnose and treat, and can also build their own front ends. Their strength makes a partnership possible while raising the bar for any paid add-on. [ADPHC premarital screening](https://www.adphc.gov.ae/en/Public-Health-Programs/Premarital-Screening); [Fakih IVF andrology](https://fakihivf.com/treatment/fertility-urology-andrology/); [CCAD urology](https://www.clevelandclinicabudhabi.ae/en/departments/urology)

“Why now” differs by buyer. For a clinic, the reason to act would be a measured access bottleneck **now**—for example a documented drop from inquiry to appointment or staff time spent on misrouted calls. The September programme launch alone is not such a bottleneck. For a DoH-aligned programme, the published 2030 roadmap and explicit shared male/female scope may justify exploring a governed access experiment, but not awarding a contract. For users, the immediate trigger is their own concern or family-planning decision, not a policy announcement. The October 2026 DoH–Cleveland Clinic Abu Dhabi–Paterna memorandum concerns research on severe male infertility; it is neither a Sitr partnership nor evidence that laboratory-grown sperm is a current care option. [Abu Dhabi Media Office, 2026](https://www.mediaoffice.abudhabi/en/health/abu-dhabi-programme-for-the-future-of-reproductive-health-an-integrated-ecosystem-shaping-standards-research-and-innovation/); [DoH, 23 October 2026](https://www.doh.gov.ae/en/news/doh-and-ccad-partner-with-paterna-biosciences-to-advance-male-fertility-research)

**Buyer-meeting decision, conditional on the baseline:** if the clinic can show a material, recurring loss between reproductive-health inquiries and appropriately attended first visits, ask its access owner and clinical sponsor to decide by their next service-planning date whether to run a 12-week comparison. Delaying that decision means the measured loss and staff handling burden continue through another planning cycle; it does not establish a clinical harm estimate. The concrete request is a de-identified four-week funnel extract, a named workflow owner, and agreement on the date and threshold for a pilot go/no-go decision. If no meaningful bottleneck appears, do not sell the pilot.

## Price and return: hypotheses that can fail

Offer a 12-week pilot for a **negotiated AED 10,000 fixed fee per site** as a pricing test, then a separate **AED 24,000/site/year fixed licence** only after stronger evidence from an expanded comparison and a buyer-specific economic case. These are hypotheses, not published tariffs or booked sales. The pilot price includes one pathway, bilingual copy review, staff training and a report, with custom integrations scoped separately; it does **not** recover the AED 75,000 live-product hardening budget on its own. The licence includes content/version management, a referral audit view, security operations and support; it does not buy clinical decisions. Avoid fees tied to tests, specialist referrals, IVF conversion or pregnancy because they would reward over-referral. A public buyer may prefer a fixed contract with quality and equity milestones. Neither price should be presented as sustainable until partner-specific cost is known.

For a clinic, the break-even logic is: **incremental appropriate attended visits × contribution per visit + verified staff-time saving − Sitr fees − implementation cost**. Contribution means net economic value after the provider's marginal clinical cost, not list-price revenue or lifetime IVF value. At an *assumed* AED 400 net contribution per incremental appropriate attended visit, an AED 24,000 annual site licence alone requires 60 such visits to break even, before the clinic's own implementation cost or staff savings. With AED 6,000 of clinic implementation expense, that becomes 75 visits. In the working capacity case, ~202 attended appointments across two sites would need ~150 incremental appropriate visits (74%) to cover two licences and two AED 6,000 implementation bills absent staff savings. At 50% incrementality, the contribution is only ~AED 40,400 versus AED 60,000 in fees and implementation: a shortfall of ~AED 19,600. The first 100 invitations yield only ~10.5 attended visits under these assumptions; even if **all** were incremental, their assumed contribution would be just AED 4,200 (10.5 × 400), far below an annual fee plus setup. This deliberately sets a demanding threshold; partner data may support a lower fee, stronger staff savings, or a no-go. No local margin or incrementality is claimed.

The programme buyer needs a different test: access, appropriate completion, patient-reported trust, equitable use, and safety compared with a simpler change to existing services. Avoid monetising presumed future births or avoided ART cycles. Seek budget ownership, purchase criteria and evidence thresholds before building a procurement projection. The user-pay path should remain deferred until interviews show that men actually want and would pay for private navigation despite existing appointment channels.

## Objections to resolve in discovery

| Objection | Response and real concession | Discovery question | Proof needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| “We already take online bookings.” | Yes. Use the current flow as the comparator; withdraw if Sitr adds no appropriate attendance or experience benefit. | Where do inquiries drop out or reach the wrong service? | Baseline funnel by channel and a controlled pilot. |
| “A questionnaire cannot assess fertility.” | Agreed. It only gathers a consented history and helps a human appointment; remove risk scores and diagnostic language. | Which questions help your team prepare without delaying care? | Clinician-approved content and audit of all user-facing outputs. |
| “Referral fees could push unnecessary consults.” | Use the fixed pilot and licence prices; no fee for a test, referral or treatment. | What over-referral rate would make this unacceptable? | Blinded clinical review of sampled referrals and documented exclusions. |
| “Sensitive data is too risky.” | Minimise collection, keep anonymous education separate, allow user review before sharing, and follow partner security architecture. | What data could stay entirely inside your system? | Privacy/security assessment, consent test and incident exercise. |
| “Men may reject this or fear exposure.” | Treat trust as a pass/fail outcome, not a branding assumption; offer a direct human route. | Which wording or channels feel safe to your patients? | Arabic/English user interviews, opt-in and abandonment by segment. |
| “The programme is public; this should be free.” | A public service can be free to users. A provider or programme pays only for an evidenced service improvement. | Who owns the access metric and its budget? | Named sponsor, procurement route and target outcome. |
| “Your volume is too small.” | Initial volume is small. Do not claim scale until repeatable site economics are shown. | What site volume and contribution would justify rollout? | Partner-supplied denominator, costs and repeat-site result. |
| “We can build this ourselves.” | Likely. Compete on measured quality and speed; license only if the managed service costs less than internal work. | What would internal delivery cost and take? | Build-versus-buy comparison including clinical governance. |

## Pilot, go-no-go and next actions

First, secure a clinical sponsor and an access owner at **one** licensed provider. Ask for de-identified baseline counts: relevant inquiries, first appointments offered, attendance, no-shows, misroutes and staff handling time over a defined period. Interview users and front-desk staff before building; include men who abandoned or avoided current booking where recruitment is ethically and practically possible. Record whether the problem is privacy, knowledge, availability, price, language or something else. A “front door” product is inappropriate if appointment capacity or cost is the main constraint.

Second, agree a protocol before any live data. Define inclusion, consent, exclusion and escalation, review every Arabic/English sentence, decide retention and host, and obtain the partner's privacy, security, clinical and ethics approvals as required. Run synthetic-case review first: prohibited diagnostic claims should be absent, clinical reviewers should find no unsafe routing, and the handoff should work when the system is unavailable. These are proposed gates, not claimed regulatory clearance.

Third, run a limited 12-week feasibility comparison against the existing route. A randomized invitation where acceptable is strongest; otherwise pre-specify a matched or phased comparison and its limits. Track **clinically appropriate attended first appointments per eligible visitor**, but report counts and uncertainty rather than claiming an incremental annual effect from the first 100 invitations. Guardrails: over-referral, safety incidents, opt-out and complaints. Secondary outcomes: completion, time to booked visit, staff minutes, trust and comprehension, and equitable performance across language and access groups. Separate “booked” from “attended”; follow up on non-attendance without exposing the concern to family or employers. Do not use pregnancy or treatment conversion as a short-pilot success claim. Use observed baseline and feasibility rates to size and pre-specify a larger prospective comparator before an annual-licence ROI decision.

The **go to expanded evaluation** decision requires a named payer, acceptable safety/privacy review, patient trust, a plausible improvement over ordinary booking and funding for continued engineering/QA. The **annual-licence go** decision requires credible incrementality and a site-level economic case after implementation cost from the larger comparison. The **no-go or redesign** decision follows material clinical misrouting, confidentiality complaints, low opt-in, no incremental attendance, no capacity to see referred patients, or no buyer willing to pay. If navigation works but appointment access is the barrier, redesign the service around scheduling and capacity rather than intensifying risk messaging.

Immediate work is limited and testable: obtain one provider's baseline and workflow owner; conduct 10–15 confidential user interviews and 5–8 staff/clinician interviews as discovery targets (not statistically representative samples); produce a reviewed clickable bilingual flow; cost a secure pilot; and request a written decision on the protocol, fee and outcome measure. Do not approach DoH with a scale claim before the provider result. No outreach, deployment, patient collection or partnership is represented as completed by this plan.
