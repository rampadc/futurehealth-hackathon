These are recommended commercial motions, not existing partnerships. A regulator's strategic interest can open a conversation but does not distribute a product, allocate a budget or complete a sale. Likewise, building an app does not acquire users: each plan names the point where a real organisation introduces it into an existing workflow.

## What we must prove to make the plan investable

For **Heat-Safe**, the buyer needs to see that the added workflow improves execution or evidence beyond its existing meter and HSE process. The business earns money through a costed site service, not a promise that every heat event generates compensation. Early sales should teach the team how to install, train and support a site; partner distribution follows a repeatable delivery package.

For **the companion**, the distribution advantage is clinic issuance to an identifiable cohort, not consumer advertising. That reduces the need to win an app-store audience, but it does not make acquisition free: sales, provider onboarding, replacement units and staff time belong in the economics. A charming device can win attention at a competition; sustained benefit and affordable support are what can earn a repeat order.

For **Sitr**, the provider already has potential users and a care pathway. Embedding the service there is a practical initial distribution hypothesis. It must generate useful access or staff-efficiency gains over ordinary booking. A consumer launch would add an unproven acquisition problem to an unproven service; it is not the first business model.

Each full plan therefore includes a first-customer sales sequence, a distribution mechanism, delivery responsibilities, a costed scenario, and expansion gates. All prices, sales conversion and future volumes remain testable assumptions. No outreach, interview, commitment or product validation is represented as completed.

## A realistic competition decision

**Provisional choice: Heat-Safe, provided the team can demonstrate a reliable decision engine and an understandable field workflow.** Its advantage is that a small prototype can show consequential operational behaviour, including a sensor fault, without relying on patient records or custom consumer hardware. Its weakness is that a heat dashboard is easy to dismiss. The demonstration must show what the supervisor does differently, why that action is justified, and what happens when the system cannot trust its inputs.

**Choose the companion instead if a working physical interaction is achievable early and the team can demonstrate bounded adaptation, not only animated rewards.** It has the most tangible presentation potential of the three. Without a physical prototype, the concept can still be explored in software, but the claim should narrow to an interaction/policy demonstration. A simulator cannot substantiate the advantage of physical presence.

**Choose Sitr instead if clinical review and culturally competent design are available early.** It has a clear human journey, but a questionnaire plus booking button is a weak technical entry. The competitive work is the governed handoff: explainable inputs, consent, clinician ownership, multilingual comprehension, and safe handling of uncertain or inappropriate requests. Without clinical review, present an educational navigation prototype and identify the missing safety evidence plainly.

These rankings are judgments about execution risk given the current archive. They are not probabilities of winning. A functioning prototype, relevant reviewer access and a credible buyer conversation can change the order. The official site publishes evaluation dimensions but no numeric weights or winning threshold. [Challenge overview](https://hack2skill.com/event/futurehealthchallenge/).

## The evidence package that makes a pitch credible

Use a short evidence chain in every presentation:

1. **A specific person and operational problem.** A supervisor making a work decision, a participant returning after a lapse, or a man seeking an appropriate consultation. Do not open with a large global market estimate.
2. **A working before-and-after demonstration.** Show the action, the underlying rule or evidence, and one failure or refusal. Clearly label synthetic inputs and simulated integrations.
3. **Measured prototype performance.** Report the number of cases, how they were selected, pass/fail definitions, failures and remaining limitations. Model tests establish behaviour on those cases; they do not establish reduced illness, sustained habits or improved fertility.
4. **A buyer and a distribution path.** Explain who signs, who introduces it to users, how it is delivered, and what the first paid engagement buys.
5. **A disciplined next experiment.** Specify the partner sought, cohort or site, duration, cost envelope, comparator, accountable owner and decision criterion. A prospective pilot design is useful even when no partner has signed.

The per-idea documents turn this structure into concrete demo sequences, evaluation cases, slide stories and judge answers. Any interview or expert feedback obtained before submission should be dated and permissioned, with the person's actual contribution distinguished from endorsement or a purchase commitment. Do not use an organisation's logo to imply a partnership.

## Rules that affect the business plan

The live terms describe an indicative seven-minute presentation and three-minute questions period, subject to organiser instructions. They also contain a six-month right-of-first-refusal provision concerning exclusive licensing/acquisition of submitted materials, with a 30-day response mechanism, and broad permissions to publicise submitted ideas. Review those provisions before submitting proprietary assets or negotiating exclusivity with a partner. The plans assume prospective, non-exclusive pilot discussions; no agreement has been accepted here. [Challenge terms](https://hack2skill.com/event/futurehealthchallenge/tnc).

A prize is upside, not operating revenue or the funding assumption for the first customer. Winning attention does not remove procurement, implementation, clinical or safety gates. The competition deliverable should make the business easier to evaluate: a convincing working example, an honest evidence record and a specific next purchase or pilot decision.
